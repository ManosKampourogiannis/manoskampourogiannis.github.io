Endogenous Family Structure and Early Childhood Development

Do children of two-parent households outperform those of single mothers because of family structure itself, or because of selection? I develop a model of parental investments in children’s skill development with endogenous family structure and estimate it using earnings, time-use, and child-outcome data from the PSID. Parents differ in their economic resources and in their preferences for leisure and child skills, which jointly determine marriage and household allocations. Selection explains 58 percent of the skill gap between children of two-parent and single-mother households, almost all of it through differences in observed family composition that operate through the child’s ability to learn. The remaining 42 percent is the effect of parents living together, which works mainly through the father’s time.

Human Capital Accumulation and Task-Biased Technological Change
with Theodosis Psomatakis

We build and estimate a general-equilibrium model of task-based production and multidimensional human capital accumulation over the life cycle. In the model, the task content of each occupation is an equilibrium outcome. This lets us recover the primitives of technological change and distinguish the effects of automation, capital deepening and new tasks on workers whose skills are built on the job.
